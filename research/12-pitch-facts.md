# 12 Pitch facts

Each fact has its source. Quotes are word for word from the page unless marked.

## Apollo walk-back

1. **"Mission rules restricted us from going more than 6 miles from the LM - the distance we could walk back in a pinch - but even so the area that could be investigated was ten times greater than before."**
   Apollo Expeditions to the Moon (NASA SP-350), chapter 14, "Touring the Moon". https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-350/ch-14-8.html
   Best single line for the hook slide. 6 miles is 9.7 km.

2. **Rover ranges stayed inside the walk-back limit.** Apollo 15: maximum range from the LM 5.0 km. Apollo 16: 4.5 km. Apollo 17: "the greatest range from the LM was 7.6 km", 35.9 km driven in total.
   https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo_lrv.html

3. **The rover already did a crude version of the Leash's maths.** "Navigation was based on continuously recording direction and distance through use of a directional gyro and odometer and inputting this data to a computer which would keep track of the overall direction and distance back to the LM." (same NSSDC page)
   Pitch: Apollo tracked distance home. EVA Sim tracks oxygen home.

4. Apollo walk-back planning: 3.3 km/h with a 30-minute reserve, average metabolic rate 234 kcal/h (numbers already in the brief; 234 kcal/h confirmed in Waligora and Horrigan 1975, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19750006325).

5. **Real walking was slower than planned.** Apollo 14 crews averaged 2.4 km/h over 2.9 km at 300 kcal/h, against the 3.3 km/h plan (same Waligora report). Good for a "plans are optimistic, so we simulate 1,000 versions" line.

## Mars delay and crew autonomy

6. **One-way light time Earth to Mars: 3.0 to 22.3 minutes.** Derived from NASA's Mars fact sheet: distance from Earth 54.6 to 401.4 million km, divided by the speed of light. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html

7. **"By the time Houston answers, you've walked 2.5 km."** At the worst-case round trip (44.6 min) and the 02 flat walking speed (0.92 m/s), a crew heading home covers about 2,450 m before a reply can arrive. At the best case (6.1 min round trip) it's about 340 m. Derived; say so on the slide.

8. **NASA already plans for crews choosing their own way home.** Joint AR ConOps (2023), section 5.1.6 "Contingency Walk-Back": EV2 "determines he is in an abort scenario and must immediately return to the lander". The crew compare paths on "the top-down map, including topography", a "line-of-sight communication coverage layer", and estimates "based on distance, slope, and terrain classification". They accept that "they should expect to lose communications for approximately 10 minutes". Mission Control only "concur[s] with the EV crew's plan" after the crew have decided.
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230016622/downloads/JointAR_ConOps_RevA_2023-11-14_Final_Signed.pdf
   This is a Moon document. The Mars version of the same problem has a 6 to 45 minute round trip on top.

9. NASA human research has a whole line of work on communication delay for Mars. Example: Karasinski et al. 2025, "Assessment of the State of Communication Delay Research in Preparation for Missions Beyond Low Earth Orbit" (NASA HRP). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250000703/downloads/2025%20HRP%20IWS%20Comm%20Delay.pdf
   No crisp one-line quote found in it; use it as a "NASA is studying this" citation, not a quote.

   Not verified tonight: a NASA Ames human factors paper reportedly saying Mars crews "will need to act with greater autonomy than any past crew" (https://humanfactors.arc.nasa.gov/publications/parisi_nasa_submission_03102022.pdf failed to load twice). Don't use the quote unless someone opens it.

## Oxygen

10. **MOXIE's best hour made about 8.4 L of O₂** (12 g/h, "twice as much as NASA's original goals"). A suited astronaut walking uses about 60 L/h (02). So one MOXIE at its best breathed for about a seventh of one walker. https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-oxygen-generating-experiment-moxie-completes-mars-mission/

11. Walking uphill on Mars costs about 3.4 times flat ground per metre at 20° (02, from Minetti 2002 scaled to Mars). One chart of this curve explains the Leash better than words.

## Past Space Apps global winners

2025 had ten global awards: Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Use of Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact. https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2025/meet-the-2025-global-winners/

Human exploration winners:

| Year | Award | Team | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Best Mission Concept | PureFlow | Mars habitat recycling systems tested "against real-time solar threats"; "always puts crew safety first" |
| 2025 | Best Use of Science | SpaceGenes+ | How radiation and microgravity combine to affect biology |
| 2023 | Best Use of Science | LunarTech Ensemble (Egypt) | "A website and immersive game" to visualise Apollo lunar seismic data (Make a Moonquake Map challenge) |
| 2023 | Best Use of Technology | Spacebee (US and Argentina) | Website combining Apollo moonquake data, locations and plots |

Source for 2023: https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/2023-nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-10-global-winners

Scale: 2024 winners were picked from 9,996 submissions at 485 events in 163 countries; 2023 from 5,556 (via search results of the NASA announcements).

What they have in common, from the blurbs:
- **Real NASA data named up front**, and the output is something a person can use or play, not just a chart. LunarTech Ensemble is the closest analogue to EVA Sim: Apollo data turned into a website plus an immersive game, and it won Best Use of Science.
- **A safety or stakes framing in one sentence** ("puts crew safety first", "testing each design against real-time solar threats").
- **Stress testing against variation**, which is what Survival Odds does.

Award fit for EVA Sim: Best Mission Concept (the Leash and ConOps link), Best Use of Science (Minetti model, sourced spreads), or Best Use of Technology (WebXR).
